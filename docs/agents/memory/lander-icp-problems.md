On 2026-10-13, Caleb split the Edible lander into two versions that share one codebase. They have the same structure and images, and only the messaging differs. A question at `/` asks which one the visitor is: the cook (parent/grandparent) or the one who misses the cooking (kid). Each version targets its ICP 100% (before this, the original site was about 80% kids and 20% parents).

**Kids, the problem:**
1. Missing the taste of home: homesick, missing their parents' food and voice.
2. Cooking for the first time on their own: they don't know how to make anything, AND they don't want to spend money eating out all the time.
3. Parents aren't going to be around forever. Kids want a memory capsule of their parents, a digital artifact to hold on to (voice, culture, stories, videos).
4. Language and distance barriers for immigrant families.
5. They want to learn to cook but are busy. Learning from parents over long phone calls and explanations takes time, and has far more friction than finding a recipe on Instagram.

**Parents, the problem:**
1. Tradition is being lost: family recipes are not being passed down.
2. They want to preserve their culture, tell the stories and pass on heritage.
3. They miss their kids but can't find the time to sync up.
4. It's hard for them to express their love.

**Big vision:** preventing families from drifting by
- bridging the knowledge gap between generations
- using mentorship to bond
- MORE THAN JUST COOKING

**Why:** these are Caleb's own notes, and he asked for them to guide the parent-ICP lander content.
**How to apply:** check every kid-version string against the kid list and every parent-version string against the parent list; the vision frames both. Also follow [[lander-copy-feedback]] (sell emotion, be instantly intuitive) and [[lander-design-references]].
