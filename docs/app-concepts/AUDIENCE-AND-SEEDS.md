# Audience notes (from the founder, Caleb) + five concept seeds

## A. The two customers (Caleb's own problem lists, Oct 13)
**Kids (the one who misses the cooking)**
1. Missing the taste of home: homesick, missing their parents' food AND their voice.
2. Cooking for the first time on their own: they don't know how to make anything, AND they don't want
   to spend money eating out all the time.
3. Parents won't be around forever. Kids want a memory capsule of their parents: a digital artifact to
   hold on to (voice, culture, stories, videos).
4. Language and distance barriers for immigrant families.
5. They want to learn to cook but are busy. Learning from parents over long phone calls takes time and
   has far more friction than a recipe on Instagram.

**Parents (the cook)**
1. Tradition is being lost: family recipes aren't being passed down.
2. They want to preserve their culture, tell the stories, pass on heritage.
3. They miss their kids but can't find the time to sync up.
4. It's hard for them to express their love. (Teaching is how they say I love you.)
5. (Added today) They are a little older and don't know tech well. Everything must be obvious.

**Big vision:** preventing families from drifting apart, by bridging the knowledge gap between
generations and using mentorship to bond. MORE THAN JUST COOKING.

**Brand line:** "Culture we want to preserve, and pass down."
Love goes both ways: parents show care by teaching; kids show intentionality & respect by asking and
cooking it back. The path is parent → kid: the parent records their own cooking and sends it to the
kid; the kid cooks it and sends a photo back.

**Caleb's standing feedback on copy & UX**
- Sell emotional intelligence, not technical complexity. Show the feature inside the emotional pain.
- Instantly intuitive or cut it. He gets lost by clever devices with no context.
- Photos must clearly show what's happening (people visibly cooking, food clearly visible).
- Keep what's loved; take it further. No B2B SaaS feel. Smooth and intuitive over flashy.
- Lander lines that landed: "Cook it like Mom does." "Are you the cook, or the one who misses it?"
  "Pass it down" / "Taste of home" stickers. "Send it home. Get one back." "Far from home." "A quieter
  kitchen." "Teaching is how they say I love you. Asking is how you say it back." "every family needs
  both" (Caveat). "They'll love it."

## B. Five concept seeds (the creative director refines these into DIRECTION.md)
All five use the same brand (Fraunces / DM Sans / Caveat, our greens, sun, peach, cream) and all apply
the eight references' structure. They differ in their lead emotion and visual system:

1. **Hearth**: the current app, grown up. Cream canvas, olive primary, yellow highlighter on one word,
   peach/rose/sage tinted cards, pot mascot. Subway-style: the hero photo fills the top half edge to
   edge with round white icon buttons over it, and a white rounded sheet overlaps its bottom edge. White
   tab bar with a clear top edge and a sage pill behind the active tab. Calm, familiar, homey.
2. **Sunday Supper**: the lander's boldness brought into the app. Chunky soft Fraunces, big color-block
   sections (forest green with chartreuse type, sun yellow, peach), swirl patterns, scalloped stickers
   ("TASTE OF HOME"), Caveat notes. The tab bar is its own forest-green block (ref 3). Joyful, festive,
   the feeling of a full Sunday table.
3. **Lamplight**: the evening kitchen. Cinematic and warm-dark (deep cocoa/forest, never cold black),
   lamplight sun-yellow accents, a full-bleed photo hero with a big title and one white pill button
   (ref 6), rounded dark bubble panels with hearts (ref 8), sun-yellow info lines (ref 7). The kid opens
   it at 11pm in a dorm and it feels like the kitchen light left on for him. The parent sub-version may
   flip to warm daylight (butter/cream) with the same structure, for legibility.
4. **Recipe Box**: nostalgia made tangible. Family recipe cards like a grandmother's index-card box,
   Polaroid-style photos, Caveat handwriting, postmark stamps ("Sent from home · Sun 12 Oct"), date
   tiles (ref 3), status pills like Luma's ("New from Mom", "You cooked it", "Mom answered"), a
   floating pill tab bar with words. Letters between home and away. Must stay clean, never cluttered.
5. **Big Table**: large print, maximum clarity, the grandparent-proof one. Built on ref 3: a solid
   colored header band ("Hi Leo"), two big photo tiles, two big full-width buttons, very few things per
   screen, giant type and buttons, a beige tab bar segmented off with big icons + words. Warmth from
   photography and words rather than decoration.
