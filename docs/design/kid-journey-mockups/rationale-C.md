# Direction C: Cook along with their voice (revised)

**Thesis:** the cook is always one tap away at the bottom of the recipe. A peach dock holds his voice and "Ask". It has one play/pause and tells you what's playing ("Miles's voice · This step · 1:12 / 3:20"). Everything you seek or skip happens in one full-screen video player. The player and the dock share the same clip and the same spot in it: open the video and you see the picture, close it and the voice keeps going. A and B lead with the step text. C leads with the cook.

**The flow, as it clicks through.** C-Home (Dad's fish) → C-FishOverview → C-NoRecording. The recorded path is C-Recipes → Weed Steak → C-Overview → C-Step. C-Overview, C-Step, C-AllSteps and C-Story are one artboard opened in different states, and so are C-FishOverview and C-NoRecording. That's why a jump, Back/Next, close and resume all land on the right step. Every Ask control opens a working chat stub with the dish or step attached: in the recipe it's part of the same artboard, and from the tab bar it's C-Ask.

**Rules this version commits to**
- The cook's words: none on the overview's ingredient list (it's a shopping and check-off list, and the "~2 stalks" vs "one" clash goes away there). Every "You need" row shows them, beside the moment he says them, with the voice one tap away. Recorded steps carry no quote text; his words are the voice and the player transcript.
- Specials always show their "why". x2 (sounds like a criticism without context) and x3 (the hackathon joke) are not shown.
- Missing facts are plain muted bracketed text, not chips: [heat: ask Miles] on steps 1, 4 and 5; [one or two? ask Miles] for green onions; [how thin: ask Miles] for apples; [amount: ask Miles] for weed oil; [rub amount: ask Dad], [degrees: ask Dad], [lid open or closed: ask Dad], [heat: ask Dad], [oil for the sauce: ask Dad].
- Text edits are deletions only: step 3 drops "Next," and "elegantly for the garnish". The fish uses the substep wording from CONTENT section 4. Transcripts are verbatim with "…" cuts; step 4's cut is the wrong smoke-point line.
